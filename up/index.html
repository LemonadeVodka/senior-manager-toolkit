Lead Up Playbook

You can't change your boss, but you can change how you work with them. That includes how you communicate, what you put in writing, how you set boundaries, and, when it's warranted, how you escalate. Leading up isn't sucking up. It's managing the most important working relationship you have, deliberately.

  • Diagnose first. Difficult, toxic, and abusive behavior call for different responses.
  • Work with their incentives, not against their personality.
  • Put it in writing. Recaps protect you and reduce "that's not what I said".
  • Escalate in order, with evidence, when the pattern doesn't change.

Playbook at a Glance

Use It WhenYour boss is hard to work with, unpredictable, or harmful, and you still have to deliver.
The Plays
  1. Diagnose the pattern
  2. Use the field guide for a brilliant but abusive boss
  3. Influence through their incentives
  4. Make your people leadership count
  5. Use scripts for hard moments
  6. Own your 1:1s
  7. Debrief every bad interaction
  8. Record, report, and counter when it's HR-reportable
  9. Escalate in order, with evidence

It's Working WhenFewer surprises, decisions confirmed in writing, calmer 1:1s, and you feel less dread before meetings with them.
Weak

React in the moment, vent to the team, keep no record, and hope it gets better.

Good

Name the pattern, adapt your communication, recap in writing, and set clear boundaries.

Best

You've earned a working rhythm, your skip-level knows your work, your record is clean, and escalation is ready if you ever need it.
