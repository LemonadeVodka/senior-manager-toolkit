Pillar 1 · Lead Up

The Influence Playbook

Classic research on managing your boss makes a simple point: the relationship is mutual dependence, and you're the one most motivated to make it work. You get influence by understanding what your boss needs and fears, and by giving them information the way they can use it.

  • Fill in the Boss Map: goals, pressures, fears, style, and hot buttons.
  • Match their style: readers get pre-reads, listeners get conversations, data people get numbers.
  • No surprises, ever. Bad news early, with a plan.
  • Confirm in writing after every decision.

Influence Moves

  1. Lead With What They Care About"This reduces the incident risk you raised with the VP" lands better than "my team needs this."
  2. Send Pre-ReadsLet a detail-oriented boss dig in privately, rather than interrogating you in public.
  3. Bring Options, Not ProblemsTwo or three options with a recommendation. Let them choose (see ARC Pushback).
  4. No SurprisesTell them bad news before anyone else does, with what you're doing about it.
  5. Make Them Look Good UpwardGive them crisp summaries they can forward. It costs you little and earns you latitude.
  6. Confirm in Writing"To confirm what we agreed today…" after every decision (template).
  7. Pick Your BattlesSpend influence on what matters most to your team and outcomes. Let small things go.

What Doesn't Work

Waiting for Them to Change

They probably won't, unless something in their environment does.

Matching Their Energy

Heat meets heat, and you lose. Stay calm and factual.

Venting to Your Team

It spreads the damage, and it will get back to them.

Silent Compliance

Saying yes to everything, then quietly missing. Use ARC instead.
