Field Guide: The Brilliant Bully

A recognizable archetype: an excellent individual engineer who believes they're the smartest person in the room, and who manages through contempt. They criticize people in public, in front of their own team and other teams. They blame quickly, call people names, demand perfection while never investing in the fundamentals, call everything a priority, and treat work-life balance as weakness. Some cross into discriminatory remarks. This guide maps each behavior to the response that actually works.

Discrimination and Abuse Aren't Management Problems

Racist or other discriminatory remarks, and name-calling, aren't "difficult style" to work around. They're conduct problems. You don't manage them. You document them, report them, and protect the people affected. As a manager, you may have a duty to report what you see, even when you weren't the target. See Skip the Ladder When.

  • Work with the expertise, never against it in public: precision, data, pre-reads.
  • Record everything: recaps, priorities, decisions, and conduct incidents.
  • Turn demands into trade-offs: force-rank priorities, and price the fundamentals.
  • Protect people first, including people on other teams who take the heat.
  • Escalate conduct. Don't absorb it.

What You Can and Can't Control

Can't Control
  • Their character or beliefs
  • Their need to be the smartest
  • Their mood in a given meeting
  • Whether leadership acts quickly
Can Control
  • The written record
  • How safe your team feels
  • Your boundaries, and your team's
  • Your reputation with peers, partners, and your skip-level
  • Your options (career insurance)

Weak / Good / Best

Weak

Absorb it, stay silent when people are insulted, accept every "priority", and work nights trying to be perfect.

Good

Pre-reads and data, force-ranked priorities, recaps in writing, repair with your team and partners, and conduct documented.

Best

Conduct is reported and on record, your team knows you'll stand up for them, partners trust your team despite the outbursts, and you have options whatever leadership decides.

Take care of yourself through this. Working under sustained contempt is exhausting. Use the Reset Protocol and the Weekly Check-In.
