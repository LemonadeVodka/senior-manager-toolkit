Scripts for Hard Moments

In a hard moment, your brain goes into threat mode and words get harder. Rehearsed lines help. Adapt these to your voice, practice them out loud, and keep them calm, short, and factual.

Upward SBI Feedback

Use it privately, once you have some credibility, and tie it to an outcome they care about.

ScriptUpward SBI
"Can I share an observation about [meeting]? In [situation], when [specific behavior], the impact was [effect on people or outcome]. I know you want [their goal] — so do I.
Would you be open to [specific alternative] next time?"

When They Criticize You in Public

ScriptIn the Moment
"That's a fair question, and I want to give you a complete answer.
Let me follow up with you directly after this meeting with the details."

Then, privately: "In the review, the discussion about my team's work got heated in front of the group. I'm happy to be challenged. Could we take detailed critiques to a 1:1, so the team stays focused?"

Impossible Deadline

ScriptARC
"I understand why this matters [Acknowledge]. With our current commitments, doing it by Friday means [X] slips or we skip [testing] [Reveal]. Options: [A], [B], or [C]. I'd recommend [B].
Which would you like? [Choose]"

The full framework is on ARC Pushback.

When They Take Credit

ScriptReclaim Gracefully
In the meeting: "Building on that — [name] on my team did the analysis behind this, and they can walk you through the data."
Afterwards, in writing: "Sharing the deck [team member] built for [project], for reference."

When They're Angry or Volatile

ScriptDe-escalate
"I can see this is frustrating. I want to fix it. Can we focus on what we need by [date]?"
If it continues: "I want to give this proper attention. Let's pick it up in 30 minutes."

When They Deny What Was Agreed

ScriptPoint to the Record
"My understanding from our [date] conversation was [X] — I sent a recap that day.
If priorities have changed, that's fine; let's confirm the new direction so I can adjust."

When They Call Someone Names

ScriptIn the Moment
"Let's keep this about the work. [Name] isn't [label] — let's look at what's actually blocking us."
If it continues: "I'm not comfortable with how this is going. Let's pause and pick it up 1:1."

Then check on the person privately the same day (repair), write it down (conduct note), and treat repeated name-calling as a conduct issue for HR (escalation).

When They Criticize Your Team in Front of Another Team

ScriptIn the Room
"There's context behind that — let me follow up with both teams after this call so we're working from the same facts."

Afterwards, send a short, professional note to the partner team's lead (template). Restate the facts, the plan, and your team's commitment. Your team's reputation shouldn't be set by someone else's outburst.

Setting a Boundary

ScriptBoundary
"I'm committed to [goal]. I do my best work when [condition — e.g. feedback is shared 1:1, requests come with a priority]. Can we agree on that going forward?"
Practice Out Loud

Say the script to a mirror or a trusted friend twice. Rehearsed words come out calm under pressure, and improvised ones usually don't.
