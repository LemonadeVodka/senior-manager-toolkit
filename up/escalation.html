The Escalation Ladder

Escalating about your own boss is high-stakes. Done well, it protects you and others. Done badly, it can damage your standing. Climb in order where you safely can, bring evidence, and know what each rung can and can't do.

Skip the Ladder If…

…there's harassment, discriminatory remarks, repeated name-calling, threats, a safety risk, or a request to break the law. Go straight to rung 3 or 4, and read When It's HR-Reportable first. Details.

Going to the Skip-Level Well

  1. Have a Relationship FirstA skip-level who already knows your work listens differently. Build it before you need it (career insurance).
  2. Frame It as a Business Problem"Our team's attrition risk and delivery are being affected by…" rather than "my boss is awful."
  3. Show What You've TriedIt shows maturity, and it's the first thing they'll ask.
  4. Make a Specific AskAdvice, coaching for your boss, a change in reporting, or simply awareness.

Before You Go to HR

  • A factual timeline from your debrief log (dates, what happened, who was present, impact).
  • Clarity on what you want: the behavior to stop, an investigation, a transfer, or advice.
  • Questions ready: How is confidentiality handled? What's the process and timeline? What protects me from retaliation?
  • A written summary sent to HR after the meeting.
  • A support person outside work: friend, partner, therapist, or lawyer.
Skip the Ladder When

There's harassment, racist or other discriminatory remarks, repeated name-calling or insults, threats, safety risks, or a request to break the law. Go directly to HR, the ethics line, or legal advice. Retaliation for raising these concerns is prohibited in many places.
