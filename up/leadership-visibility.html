Make Leadership Count in a Technical Culture

In some organizations only technical skill gets noticed. People leadership is treated as soft, optional, or a sign you couldn't cut it as an engineer. The work still matters. It's just invisible unless you translate it into the language the culture already respects: systems, numbers, and outcomes.

Lead With the Number, Then the Story

"MTTR dropped from 52 to 31 minutes" gets attention in a technical culture. "Because the team now escalates early instead of hiding problems" is the leadership story, and it lands once the number has earned the hearing.

Report: The Monthly Impact Note

A short email to your skip-level (and your boss, if that's safe) once a month: metrics first, people story second, an ask last. It builds the visibility your boss isn't giving you, and it creates a dated record of your contribution.

  1. Three NumbersPick metrics from the table that moved, with last month's value for comparison.
  2. One StoryThe leadership move behind one of the numbers, in two sentences.
  3. One Risk, One AskWhat could slip, and the decision or support you need.

Prove: Earn Technical Standing

You don't need to out-engineer your engineers. You do need to show you think rigorously, so your people leadership isn't mistaken for a lack of technical depth.

  • Use PROVE in every design review. Precise questions about risk, rollback, and value signal rigor.
  • Own a decision log. A dated record of the technical decisions your team made, the options, and why. It shows judgment over time, and it protects you when someone rewrites history.
  • Learn the 30 core terms (vocabulary) well enough to follow the debate and ask the next question.
  • Put your engineers in front of the room. Their technical credibility rubs off on the team you built.

At Review Time

  • Collect your twelve Monthly Impact Notes. They're your self-review, already written.
  • Pull your wins file and quotes from peers or partner teams.
  • Frame your people outcomes as business risk avoided: attrition, incidents, missed commitments.
  • Ask your skip-level directly what "exceeds" looks like for a people leader here, and get the answer in writing.
  • If your boss writes your review and there's an open HR concern, keep copies of everything (retaliation watch).
