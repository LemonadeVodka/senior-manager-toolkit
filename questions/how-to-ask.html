Pillar 4 · Lead With Questions

How to Ask

The same question can sound like curiosity or like an accusation. How you ask decides whether engineers open up or get defensive, and whether you build credibility or lose it.

Curious, Not Interrogating

Interrogating
  • "Why didn't you think of X?"
  • "Is this going to break again?"
  • "Who signed off on this?"
Curious
  • "Help me understand how X fits in."
  • "What would tell us early if this starts to fail?"
  • "How did we arrive at this decision?"

The Follow-Up Ladder

Start broad, then climb only as far as you need to.

  1. Show Me"Can you show me the data, or walk me through it?"
  2. What's Normal?"What does this usually look like? Compared with what?"
  3. Where Exactly?"Which part of the system, which customers, which step?"
  4. What Else Could It Be?"What other explanations did you rule out, and how?"
  5. What Would You Change?"If you owned this completely, what would you do?"

Admit What You Don't Know

"I'm not deep on this. Explain it like I'm going to brief the VP." This is a strength move. It invites clear thinking, models honesty, and is far better than nodding along and getting caught out later.

Close the Loop

Write down what you asked. A week later: "Last week you mentioned the rollback wasn't tested. Where did that land?" Remembering is what turns a good question into lasting credibility.
