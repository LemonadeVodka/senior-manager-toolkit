Exiting Well

If you decide to leave, leave in a way you'll be proud of. Your reputation travels further than your current boss, and your team will remember how you handled it.

The Exit Plan

  1. Plan QuietlyDon't announce your intentions, or vent about leaving at work. Search on your own time and devices.
  2. Secure the Offer FirstResign after you have a signed offer, unless your health requires otherwise.
  3. Resign NeutrallyShort, gracious, and in writing (template). This isn't the moment for grievances.
  4. Hand Over CleanlyDocument your work, introduce successors, and finish what you can. Make it easy for your team.
  5. Protect Your TeamTell them personally, before the wider announcement if you can. Advocate for their next manager and their growth.
  6. Decide About the Exit InterviewFactual, constructive feedback can help the people who stay. Keep it professional, specific, and focused on impact. If you've already raised formal concerns, refer to them.
  7. Keep the BridgesThank people who helped you, and stay in touch with allies. Industries are small.
Leaving Isn't Failing

Choosing to leave a harmful situation is a leadership decision about your own life. Many strong leaders have done it, and done better afterwards.
