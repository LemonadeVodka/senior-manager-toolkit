Pillar 6 · Lead Yourself

The Reset Protocol

You just had a bad interaction. Your heart's racing, your jaw is tight, and a reply is forming in your head. Stop. The next ten minutes decide whether this stays one bad moment or becomes two. Follow these steps in order.

The Protocol

  1. Pause (1 Minute)Step away from the screen. Stand up. You don't have to respond right now.
  2. Breathe (2 Minutes)Slow breathing with a longer exhale, for example in for 4, out for 6, repeated ten times. It signals safety to your nervous system.
  3. No Reply for 20 MinutesDon't send, post, or vent in writing. If something is truly urgent, a holding reply is fine: "Got it, I'll come back to you shortly."
  4. Write the Facts (3 Minutes)What happened, the exact words, and who was there. Use the Interaction Debrief. Getting it out of your head helps you stop replaying it.
  5. Reframe (2 Minutes)Ask: "What's the one fair point, if any? What was about them, not me? What would I tell a friend in this situation?"
  6. Choose Your Next Move (2 Minutes)Do nothing, follow up in writing, use a script, check on your team, or talk to someone you trust.

A Physical Reset

If you can: a short walk outside, cold water on your face, or a few minutes of movement. Stress lives in the body. Moving helps clear it faster than thinking does.

At the End of the Day

  • Write one thing that went well today, however small.
  • Close the laptop and do a transition ritual: a walk, music, or a change of clothes.
  • Don't check messages from your boss in the evening, unless you're on call.
  • If you're still replaying it at night, write it down and tell yourself you'll deal with it tomorrow.
Not Every Battle Needs a Response

Often the most powerful move is a calm, factual follow-up the next day, or nothing at all. Responding when you're calm, rather than reacting in the moment, is what leaders do.
