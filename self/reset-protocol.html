The Reset Protocol

You just had a bad interaction. Your heart's racing, your jaw is tight, and a reply is forming in your head. Stop. The next ten minutes decide whether this stays one bad moment or becomes two. Three steps: Pause, Write, Choose.

The Protocol

  1. Pause (3 Minutes)Step away from the screen and stand up. Breathe slowly with a longer exhale: in for 4, out for 6, ten times. Send nothing for 20 minutes: no reply, no post, no venting in writing.
  2. Write (4 Minutes)The facts: what happened, the exact words, who was there. Use the Interaction Debrief. Then one line each: What's the fair point, if any? What was about them, not me? Getting it on paper stops the replay, and it builds your record.
  3. Choose (3 Minutes)Pick one move: do nothing, follow up in writing tomorrow, use a script, check on your team, or talk to someone you trust. If it crossed a line (discriminatory remarks, threats, name-calling), the move is When It's HR-Reportable.

A Physical Reset

If you can: a short walk outside, cold water on your face, or a few minutes of movement. Stress lives in the body. Moving helps clear it faster than thinking does.

At the End of the Day

  • Write one thing that went well today, however small.
  • Close the laptop and do a transition ritual: a walk, music, or a change of clothes.
  • Don't check messages from your boss in the evening, unless you're on call.
  • If you're still replaying it at night, write it down and tell yourself you'll deal with it tomorrow.
Not Every Battle Needs a Response

Often the most powerful move is a calm, factual follow-up the next day, or nothing at all. Responding when you're calm, rather than reacting in the moment, is what leaders do.
