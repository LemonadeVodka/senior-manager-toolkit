Pillar 3 · Lead the Room

Purpose First

Most bad meetings fail before they start, because nobody knows what the meeting is for. Before you send or accept an invite, name its purpose. Each purpose has its own right format, length, and guest list.

The Four Purposes

DDecideMake a choice. Needs options, a decider, and data in advance.
DDiscussExplore a problem or generate ideas. Needs diverse voices and a facilitator.
IInformShare updates. Usually better async, in writing or a short video.
CConnectBuild relationships and trust: 1:1s, team rituals, skip-levels.

Decide3–7, including the decider25–50 minA pre-read with options and a recommendationA decision and an owner
Discuss4–845–60 minA clear question and a facilitatorOptions, or the next step
InformAny, async0 min (written)A clear summaryQuestions answered in the thread

The Rules

  • No purpose, no meeting. Put the purpose word in the invite title: "DECIDE: Q4 vendor renewal".
  • Inform goes async, unless it's sensitive or emotional (a reorg, layoffs).
  • Decide meetings need a decider in the room. If they can't come, move the meeting.
  • Decline politely when your role isn't clear: "What would you like me to contribute? If it's an update, I'm happy to read the notes."
