The Laws in Practice

Greene's book presents 48 laws through historical stories. Rather than listing them, this page groups their recurring ideas into seven workplace themes. Each theme has a white-magic use you can practise ethically, the dark use you should recognize, and an example with a difficult boss.

Seven Power Themes

1. Managing Superiors

The Idea

People above you can feel threatened by subordinates who seem to outshine them.

White Magic

Give your boss credit upward, frame your ideas as building on theirs, and make them look good, while building your visibility elsewhere.

Dark Use

A boss who suppresses talented people to avoid being overshadowed. Recognize it, and protect your visibility.

Example: instead of correcting your technical boss in front of executives, you send them the data beforehand. They present the insight, and the executive later asks who did the analysis.

2. Reputation

The Idea

Reputation is a cornerstone of power. Guard it, and it works for you.

White Magic

Be known for something valuable: calm in crises, reliable delivery, honest reporting.

Dark Use

Attacking a rival's reputation through rumor. Counter it with facts and visibility (counter).

3. Attention and Visibility

The Idea

Being unseen is a disadvantage. Visibility attracts opportunity.

White Magic

Demo your team's work, send crisp outcome updates, and speak at internal forums. Show the results, not yourself.

Dark Use

Grandstanding, or taking the spotlight from others' work.

4. Restraint and Information

The Idea

Saying less and revealing intentions selectively keeps you from being predictable or exposed.

White Magic

Discretion: don't over-explain, don't leak plans before they're ready, and keep confidences.

Dark Use

Deliberate deception about intentions. Recognize it, and confirm agreements in writing.

5. Dependence

The Idea

The more others rely on you, the more secure your position.

White Magic

Own capabilities and relationships that create real value, while documenting and developing successors.

Dark Use

Hoarding knowledge to become irreplaceable, or making others dependent so they can't leave.

6. Timing and Action

The Idea

Act at the right moment, and win through actions, not arguments.

White Magic

Pilot before you propose, demo before you debate, and raise asks when people are receptive.

Dark Use

Ambushing people in meetings, or striking when a rival is vulnerable.

7. Alliances and Adversaries

The Idea

Choose allies carefully, and be wary of both friends and enemies.

White Magic

Build broad coalitions on shared interests, and stay professional with everyone, including adversaries.

Dark Use

Divide-and-conquer, isolating rivals, or "crushing" opponents. Recognize it and counter it. Never practise it.

Recognize Only

Some of the book's ideas depend on deception, manipulation, cruelty, or destroying rivals. They fail the Daylight Test. Learn them so you can spot them and defend yourself, and never use them on others.
