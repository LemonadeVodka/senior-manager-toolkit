Counter-Spells

When someone is playing power games against you, often your own boss, you need to recognize the pattern quickly and respond calmly. Each counter-spell has three parts: recognize, respond, and record.

The Counters

Power playRecognizeRespondRecord
Credit-takingYour work appears in their deck with no mention of youPut names on work, demo directly, send outcome updates to wider audiences, build skip-level visibilityKeep dated versions and emails showing authorship
Isolation from executivesYou're dropped from meetings and threads, and everything routes through themAsk directly to be included ("I'd like to present my team's work"). Build peer and skip-level relationships.Note exclusions with dates
Divide-and-conquerDifferent stories told to you and your peers, and peers turned against each otherTalk to peers directly and compare notes calmly. Align on shared facts.Confirm agreements in group threads
Moving goalpostsSuccess criteria change after you deliverAgree criteria in writing up front, and when they change: "Happy to adjust. This replaces the earlier goal."Keep the original written agreement
Public underminingDismissive remarks about you or your team in front of othersStay calm, take it offline, then follow up privately with upward SBI (script)Log it in the Interaction Debrief
Information hoardingYou're missing context others have, then you're blamed for itAsk explicitly ("What do I need to know about X?"), and find other sources. Document your requests.Save your requests and the replies

When You Plan to Report

If the conduct is HR-reportable, every counter-spell has a second job: it must strengthen your complaint, not weaken it. The Record column above is your evidence, so keep it factual, dated, and off company devices where policy allows. Skip any move that could be read as retaliation. See Countering While a Case Is Open.

Counter-Spell Principles

  • Calm beats clever. The person who stays composed looks like the leader.
  • Written beats spoken. Recaps are your strongest shield.
  • Allies beat isolation. Power plays work best on people who are alone.
  • Don't retaliate in kind. Fighting dark with dark makes you the problem.
