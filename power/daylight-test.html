The Daylight Test

Power without a line turns you into the boss you're trying to survive. Before you use any move from this pillar, ask three questions: Seen? True? Fair? If it fails any of them, don't do it.

The Test

  1. Seen?Would I be comfortable if my team, my boss's boss, and HR saw exactly what I did and why?
  2. True?Is it free of lies, deception, and false impressions?
  3. Fair?Does it avoid unfair harm to anyone's reputation or work, stay within law and company policy, and protect (or at least not hurt) my team?
When You Plan to Report: Is It Case-Safe?

If you're documenting a boss's conduct or have filed a complaint, add a fourth check: would this strengthen my complaint, or weaken it? A clean record, calm recaps, and allies strengthen it. Anything that looks like retaliation, leaking, or venting gives the other side a story. See Countering While a Case Is Open.

Passes and Fails

Passes
  • Pre-wiring a decision with allies
  • Timing an ask for after a big win
  • Building skip-level visibility through results
  • Saying less in a negotiation
  • Recapping decisions in writing
Fails
  • Spreading rumors about a rival
  • Hoarding information to make someone fail
  • Pretending to agree, then sabotaging
  • Taking credit for your team's work
  • Secretly recording colleagues

Why It Matters

Ethical power compounds. People trust you more over time, alliances deepen, and your reputation precedes you. Ruthless power works until it's discovered, and then it collapses, usually at the worst moment. You're playing a long game.
