Pillar 7 · Go on Offense

The SPELL Framework

Five moves that build and use power at work, distilled from recurring themes in The 48 Laws of Power and adapted to pass the Daylight Test. Use them deliberately, not by accident.

SPELL

SSay LessStrategic restraint PPositionReputation and visibility EEarn LeverageBe essential L1Line Up AlliesCoalitions first L2Land on TimingAct when it counts

Every move must pass the Daylight Test (Seen? True? Fair?). If you're documenting a boss's conduct, it must also be case-safe.

S · Say Less

People who talk too much reveal their plans, overcommit, and give others ammunition. Restraint reads as confidence.

  • Don't over-explain when you push back. State the trade-off and stop (ARC).
  • Don't reveal plans early, such as a reorg idea, a job search, or an escalation, before they're ready.
  • Let silence work in negotiations. The first person to fill it often concedes.
  • Don't complain upward or sideways. Complaints travel, and they make you look weak.
Instead of a five-minute defense: "I hear the concern. I'll come back to you with a plan by Thursday."

P · Position

Your reputation arrives in the room before you do. Guard it, and shape it deliberately.

  • Make your boss look good in public. Insecure leaders punish people who outshine them, so give them credit upward while you build your own visibility through other channels.
  • Build skip-level and peer visibility through outcomes: crisp updates, demos, solved problems.
  • Own your narrative. Tell your team's story before someone else tells it for you, especially after incidents.
  • Be consistent. A reputation for reliability and calm is a powerful asset.

E · Earn Leverage

Power flows to people others depend on. Become essential through value, not through hoarding.

  • Own a critical capability: the relationship with a key vendor, the reliability metrics executives trust, the team that fixes the hard problems.
  • Be the translator between engineering and executives. Few people can do both (Lead With Questions).
  • Solve your boss's biggest problem. Value to them buys latitude for you.
  • Never hoard information or create single points of failure. That's dark leverage, and it collapses when discovered.

L1 · Line Up Allies

Decisions are usually made before the meeting. Coalitions beat brilliance.

  • Pre-wire every important decision 1:1 before the room.
  • Bank favors. Help peers before you need them: share resources, give credit, unblock.
  • Find shared interests with other leaders who suffer from the same problem.
  • Build relationships in every direction: up, sideways, down, and outside.

L2 · Land on Timing

The same request can fail on Monday and succeed on Thursday. Timing is a lever.

  • Ask when they're receptive: after a win, not during a crisis or a bad mood.
  • Use moments of change, such as a new budget cycle, a reorg, or a post-incident review, to push for what you need.
  • Win with results, not arguments. Pilot it, demo it, show the data, rather than debating in the abstract.
  • Be patient when the moment isn't right, and prepared when it is.
