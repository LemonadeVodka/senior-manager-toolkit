Pillar 5 · Lead Your Team

Team Safety Pulse

Psychological safety was studied extensively by Amy Edmondson, and it shows up in research on effective teams. You can't manage what you don't measure. A five-minute anonymous pulse each quarter tells you whether the pressure above is reaching your team.

The Pulse (1–5, Strongly Disagree → Strongly Agree)

These statements are written in the spirit of Edmondson's team psychological safety measure, adapted for a quick pulse. Run them anonymously in any survey tool.

  1. If I make a mistake on this team, it isn't held against me.
  2. I can bring up problems and tough issues.
  3. People on this team don't reject others for being different.
  4. It's safe to take a risk on this team.
  5. It's easy to ask other members of this team for help.
  6. No one on this team would deliberately undermine my efforts.
  7. My unique skills and talents are valued and used.
  8. Optional: Pressure from outside our team affects how safe I feel speaking up.

Running It Well

  • Use a truly anonymous tool. Don't show results for groups smaller than five people.
  • Explain why: "I want to know if our team feels safe, so I can fix what's not working."
  • Share the results with the team, including the bad ones.
  • Pick one thing to improve, and say what you'll do.
  • Repeat quarterly and watch the trend, not the absolute number.

Acting on Results

Low score on…Try
Mistakes held against peopleBlameless reviews, and share your own mistakes first
Raising problemsAsk for bad news explicitly, and thank the messenger publicly
Taking risksName safe-to-try experiments, and celebrate learning from failure
Outside pressureStrengthen the umbrella (how), and repair after incidents with leadership
