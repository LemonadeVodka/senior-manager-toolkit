Rebuilding a Damaged Team

One bad meeting needs a repair conversation. Months of a toxic director leave something deeper: people who've stopped volunteering ideas, stopped disagreeing, and started quietly interviewing. You can't remove the cause on your own, but you can rebuild the team underneath it. It takes about 90 days, and it starts with you being predictable.

Signs the Team Is Damaged

  • Meetings are silent until you speak, and nobody disagrees with you or with each other.
  • People ask permission for things they used to just do.
  • Mistakes get hidden or blamed, and problems surface late, as incidents.
  • Your strongest people have stopped presenting, or have started taking long lunches and short calls.
  • Jokes about leadership have turned bitter.

The 90 Days

  1. Stabilize (Weeks 1–4)Be the predictable one. Same rituals at the same times, decisions explained, no surprises. Strengthen the umbrella: take the director's heat yourself, and pass on only the clarity. Tell the team plainly what you will and won't tolerate in how people are treated, and then act on it every time (when a report is targeted).
  2. Restore (Weeks 5–8)Small visible wins: pick work the team can finish and show. Give public credit by name, in writing, to audiences above the director where you can (how). Run blameless reviews and share your own mistakes first. Ask for bad news and thank the person who brings it.
  3. Rebuild (Weeks 9–12)Hand back autonomy and ownership. Give people decisions, not just tasks. Restart stretch work, growth conversations, and presenting, with you in the room as backup. Let people disagree with you in public, and visibly change your mind when they're right.

Repairing as a Group

Name it once, briefly, without trashing the director. People need to know you've seen it. They don't need you to join the complaint.

ScriptTeam Reset
"I know the last few months have been hard on this team. Some of what's happened in reviews hasn't been okay, and I've seen it.
I can't control everything above us. Here's what I can control, and what you can count on from me:
- I'll take the heat in reviews. You won't be blindsided in front of other teams.
- If you're treated badly, tell me, and I'll act on it.
- Mistakes here get a blameless review, not a blame hunt.
- Your work gets credited by name.
Hold me to that."

More on how far to go: Talking About Your Boss.

Measure the Trend

Run the Team Safety Pulse anonymously every two weeks during the rebuild, then quarterly. Record each pulse's averages in the tracker on that page, and it will show what changed since the last one. Watch the direction, not the absolute number. Share the results, including the bad ones, and pick one thing to fix each time.

You Can't Out-Shield a Pattern Forever

An umbrella has limits. If the director keeps targeting your people, team protection becomes an escalation issue: log it, and use the Escalation Ladder or When It's HR-Reportable. Protect your own capacity too (rebuilding confidence).
