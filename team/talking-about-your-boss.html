Talking About Your Boss

Your team sees what's happening. Pretending everything's fine destroys your credibility, and trashing your boss destroys your professionalism. The path between is loyal without lying: acknowledge reality, focus on what you control, and keep confidences.

Principles

  • Acknowledge the experience, not the character: "That meeting was rough" rather than "they're impossible".
  • Explain context where it helps: "There's a lot of pressure from the exec team on incident numbers."
  • Focus on what you control: "Here's what I'm doing about it, and here's what we can do."
  • Never share confidential issues about your boss, such as performance, HR matters, or your own escalations.

Answering Hard Questions

They askYou say
"Why do they treat us like this?""I don't think it's personal, and it isn't okay either. I'm raising the impact with them. In the meantime, bring things to me first."
"Are you okay?""It's a tough stretch, and I'm handling it. Thanks for asking. My job is to make sure you're okay."
"Should I go to HR?""If you feel you've been treated unfairly, that's your right, and I'll support you. Do you want to talk it through first?"
"Are you leaving?""I'm focused on this team and our work." Don't lie if you've decided, and don't announce before you're ready.
Don't Recruit Your Team

Your conflict with your boss isn't theirs to fight. Never ask your team to take sides, gather evidence, or join an escalation. If they experience mistreatment themselves, support them in raising it in their own right.
