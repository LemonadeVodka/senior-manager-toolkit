Pillar 5 · Lead Your Team

Repair After Damage

When your boss humiliates a presenter, dismisses a team's work, or blows up in a meeting, the damage spreads fast. People withdraw, stop volunteering, and start updating their resumes. A quick, genuine repair limits the damage and shows your team that you've got their back.

The Repair Conversation (Same Day)

  1. Reach Out Privately, Quickly"Got 10 minutes? I wanted to check in after the review."
  2. Acknowledge What Happened"That was harder than it should have been. I'm sorry you were on the receiving end."
  3. Separate Tone From Substance"The delivery wasn't okay. There's one fair point underneath it, about the timeline, which we can address."
  4. Reaffirm Their Value"Your analysis was solid, and I want you presenting again."
  5. Ask What They Need"What would help: me presenting next time with you, prep together, or something else?"
  6. Follow Up Upward if NeededUse upward SBI with your boss about the impact.

Team-Level Repair

After a damaging moment in front of many people, address it with the team at your next touchpoint, briefly and without trashing your boss: "Tuesday's review was tough. I want to be clear: the work was strong, and I'm proud of it. We'll take the one valid point about timing and move on."

What Not to Say

"That's Just How They Are."

It normalizes the behavior and tells your team you won't do anything.

"They're a Jerk."

It feels good for a moment. It will get back to them, and it makes you less trustworthy.

"Toughen Up."

It puts the problem on the person who was harmed.
