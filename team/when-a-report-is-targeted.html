When a Report Is Targeted

Sometimes a difficult boss fixates on one person: repeated public criticism, exclusion, unrealistic demands, or attempts to manage them out. This is where your leadership matters most. Protecting them is your duty, and the way you do it protects you too.

The Plan

  1. Intervene in the Moment"I'll take that one" or "Let's take it offline" (lines). Don't leave them alone in the spotlight.
  2. Check In PrivatelyUse the repair conversation. Ask how they're doing, and believe them.
  3. Separate Fact From BiasIs there a real performance issue underneath? If so, address it fairly yourself, with your own evidence, so it isn't driven by your boss's fixation.
  4. Protect Their Work and ReputationMake their contributions visible, with names on work, demos, and credit in your updates.
  5. DocumentNote specific incidents factually in your own records (how).
  6. Raise It UpwardUse upward SBI with your boss about the impact on the team and results. If it continues, use the escalation ladder.
  7. Know When It's HR's BusinessIf the targeting relates to protected characteristics, involves retaliation, or harms their health, HR needs to know. Support your report in raising it, and know that you may have a duty to report it yourself.

When the Remarks Are Racist or Discriminatory

  1. Act the Same DayEven if you weren't the target, and even if the person affected says "it's fine."
  2. Check on the PersonPrivately: "What was said wasn't okay. How are you? What would you like to happen?" Respect their wishes where you can, and be honest about any duty you have to report.
  3. Write It DownExact words, date, who was present, who was affected (conduct incident note).
  4. Report ItTo HR or the ethics line. In many organizations, managers are expected or required to report discriminatory conduct they witness.
  5. Watch for RetaliationAgainst the person affected, and against you. Document any change in treatment after a report.
Don't Stay Silent

Silence reads as agreement, to the target, to the team, and later to an investigator. You don't have to win every battle, but you do have to be seen standing next to your person.
