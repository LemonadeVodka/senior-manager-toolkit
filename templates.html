Private feedback to your boss, tied to their goals. See Scripts.

Written HR Complaint

Factual and pattern-based. Attach your timeline and keep a copy. See When It's HR-Reportable.

EmailHR Complaint
Subject: Formal concern about conduct — [your name], [team]

Hi [HR partner],

I'm raising a formal concern about a pattern of conduct by [name, role] toward [me / members of my team] between [start date] and [end date].

Summary: [One or two sentences, e.g. "Repeated name-calling and a discriminatory remark in team meetings, and public humiliation of team members in reviews."]

Key incidents (full timeline attached):
- [Date] — [setting] — [what was said or done, exact words] — witnesses: [names]
- [Date] — [ ]
- [Date] — [ ]

Impact: [on me, on my team, on the work — e.g. two engineers have stopped presenting; one has asked to transfer]

What I've done so far: [e.g. raised it directly on (date); discussed with skip-level on (date)]

What I'm asking for: [e.g. the behavior to stop; an investigation; protection from retaliation]

Please confirm you've received this, how confidentiality will be handled, what the process and timeline are, and how I'm protected from retaliation.

Thank you,
[Name]

Fundamentals Gap Memo

When the standard demanded is higher than the investment made. Puts the trade-off on record.

MemoFundamentals Gap
Subject: What it takes to meet [standard]

The standard: [e.g. zero customer-found incidents / sub-second P95 / flawless launches]

Where we are: [current state, with one or two numbers]

The gaps holding us back:
- Tooling: [e.g. no automated testing for X]
- Observability: [e.g. dashboards cover 10% of services]
- Skills/training: [ ]
- Staffing/time: [e.g. on-call load leaves no project time]

What closing them takes: [investment, people, time] — options A (full) / B (phased)
Risk if we don't: [likely incidents, slips, attrition]

I recommend [option]. Can we decide by [date]?

Lead Your Team

Stay Conversation

Ask before people decide to leave. See Rebuilding a Damaged Team.

ScriptStay Conversation
"I want to make sure this team is a place you want to stay. Can I ask you a few questions?
- What keeps you here?
- What would make you think about leaving?
- What's the hardest part of your work right now?
- If you could change one thing about how we work, what would it be?
- What do you need from me that you're not getting?"

Listen. Don't defend. Then: "Thank you. Here's the one thing I'm going to do about this — and I'll check back with you by [date]."
