Pillar 2 · Lead Your Capacity

Stop Multitasking

Multitasking feels productive and is exhausting. Every switch between tasks costs attention and leaves part of your mind on the last thing. For a manager pulled in ten directions, the fix isn't more discipline. It's fewer things in flight and a structure that protects focus.

  • Limit your work in progress: a Weekly Big 3.
  • Separate focus from availability: focus blocks and office hours.
  • Batch the small stuff: messages, approvals, email.
  • Delegate to grow others, not just to unload.

The Context-Switching Tax

Each switch means re-loading context: where was I, what did we decide, what's next? Research on task switching consistently shows slower, more error-prone work, and a lingering "attention residue" from the previous task. Ten switches an hour can leave you busy all day and finished with nothing.

The Weekly Big 3

  1. Monday, 15 MinutesChoose the three outcomes that would make this week a success. Outcomes, not activities.
  2. Block Time FirstPut two or three focus blocks for them in your calendar before anything else lands there.
  3. Say Them Out LoudShare them with your boss and team. It makes ARC easier when new asks arrive.
  4. Friday, 5 MinutesDid you finish them? If not, what got in the way? That's your data for next week's trade-offs.

Focus Blocks vs Office Hours

Focus Blocks
  • 60–90 minutes, two or three times a week
  • Notifications off, status set
  • One task only
  • Protected like a meeting with your boss
Office Hours
  • Daily 30-minute open slot
  • Your team knows to bring questions here
  • Fewer drive-by interruptions all day
  • Urgent issues still come straight to you

Batching and the Notification Diet

  • Check chat and email at set times (for example, 9:00, 12:00, 16:00), not continuously.
  • Turn off notifications for every channel except direct messages and incidents.
  • Batch approvals, reviews, and sign-offs into one daily slot.
  • Mute large channels. Ask your team to tag you if it's truly needed.
  • Close the chat app during focus blocks. The world will survive 90 minutes.

Delegate to Grow People

Ask of every task: "Who on my team would grow from owning this?" Delegate the outcome, not the steps, and agree check-ins. You get time back, and they get a stretch assignment. Keep things that only you can do: decisions, relationships with executives, and protecting the team.
