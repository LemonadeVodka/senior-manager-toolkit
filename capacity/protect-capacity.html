Protect Your Capacity

Time is only half of capacity. The other half is energy. A difficult boss, constant context switching, and back-to-back meetings drain it fast. Protecting capacity means managing both deliberately, before they run out.

The Energy Budget

For one week, mark every recurring activity as + (gives energy), 0 (neutral), or − (drains). Then:

CategoryTypical examplesMove
Drains you can dropStatus meetings you don't need to be in, reports nobody readsDrop, delegate, or make async
Drains you can't drop1:1s with a difficult boss, escalationsSchedule them before a "+" activity, and never back-to-back
NeutralRoutine approvalsBatch them
EnergizersCoaching your team, solving real problems, deep workProtect and schedule them, especially after drains

Calendar Defense

  • Default meetings to 25 or 50 minutes, so there's a buffer.
  • Block a lunch break and protect it.
  • Put focus blocks in first each week (Weekly Big 3).
  • Decline meetings with no agenda or purpose (Purpose First).
  • One meeting-free half day a week, if your culture allows it.
  • Set working hours in your calendar, and stop accepting invites outside them.

Plan Recovery

After an intense stretch (a major incident, a reorg, a brutal review), schedule recovery the way you'd schedule the work: a lighter day, a late start, real time off. Athletes plan recovery. Leaders who don't, burn out. See Recovery & Support.
